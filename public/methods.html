Conforma is a protein workspace for public coordinates, local files and idealized sequence construction. Its original study compares two published X-ray structures of Escherichia coli adenylate kinase. It is an educational structural comparison, with a numerical engine you can inspect and reproduce.

Your own structures and sequences

The protein workspace opens supported PDB/mmCIF coordinate files, searches RCSB by name or ID, and downloads the original source bytes. Local files are parsed in a worker on your device. The displayed model retains protein heavy atoms, with chain selection; water, ligands, nucleic acids, later models and most non-A alternate locations are omitted. File and display limits are listed in the workspace. There is no biological-assembly expansion.

Sequence construction uses the supplied amino-acid labels and chosen ideal backbone torsions. Optional side chains are rigidly fitted RCSB Chemical Component Dictionary ideal-coordinate templates, not sequence-specific predictions. Backbone bond lengths (Å): N–Cα 1.458, Cα–C 1.525, C–N 1.329, C–O 1.229. Bond angles (degrees): N–Cα–C 111.2, Cα–C–N 116.2, C–N–Cα 121.7, Cα–C–O 120.8. Peptide ω is fixed at 180°. These are declared construction parameters, not measurements of the entered protein.

Ideal component geometry can differ from the backbone anchors. Fitting residuals are retained with the construction record. Side-chain rotamers, steric interactions, ring closure, terminal caps, protonation and residue-specific torsion constraints are not optimized or validated. A constructed helix or strand is not a predicted native fold, an energy-minimized model, or experimental evidence.

Two experimental observations in the original study

  • 4AKE: unbound adenylate kinase, chain A. The bundled coordinate file reports 2.20 Å resolution.
  • 1AKE: adenylate kinase bound to AP5A, a substrate-mimicking inhibitor, chain A. The bundled coordinate file reports 2.00 Å resolution. The original paper’s title reports refinement at 1.9 Å; Conforma displays the value in its retrieved PDB record.

The project includes the raw PDB files, retrieval timestamps, URLs and SHA-256 hashes. Data are downloaded during preparation, not fetched from an AI service. Browser exploration uses the bundled snapshot. A hash detects changes; it does not establish scientific authenticity on its own.

What is included

Only heavy atoms from standard ATOM records in chain A of the first model are retained. Blank or A alternate locations are accepted, with blank preferred for a duplicate atom key. HETATM records, including water and inhibitor atoms, are not displayed. Atoms are paired by residue number, insertion code, atom name, and residue identity.

This pair has 214 matched Cα residues and 1,656 common heavy atoms. Missing or unmatched atoms are excluded rather than filled in. There is no general sequence alignment, alternate-conformer population model, crystallographic symmetry expansion, structure prediction, or user-recording upload.

Portable workspaces

Save workspace includes original coordinate text and view settings. Reopening checks the coordinate SHA-256 and parses the source again. Constructed models must reproduce their coordinates from the saved recipe and bundled templates. Saved metadata does not authenticate provenance. Version 1 records cannot be reopened. Private records may contain your sequence and filename; share them deliberately. Camera orientation and selected atoms are not restored.

Why alignment matters

A closed structure is rigidly rotated and translated onto the open structure. The default fit uses the 133 Cα atoms in the CORE region. The whole-chain alternative fits all 214 matched Cα atoms. Rotation is proper: it does not reflect the molecule.

The JavaScript implementation uses Horn’s quaternion solution, solving its symmetric 4×4 eigenproblem with Jacobi rotations. Domain and per-residue displacements are then measured in this chosen reference frame. A domain RMSD is the square root of the mean squared displacement of its Cα atoms after that fit. It is not a separate best fit of the domain.

With the bundled data and CORE fit, fit-region RMSD is 1.5508 Å, all-chain Cα RMSD is 8.0940 Å, and LID-domain RMSD is 14.8687 Å. Whole-chain fitting yields 7.1307 Å for its own fit, illustrating why the alignment definition belongs with every result.

The domain convention

Conforma follows the domain definitions used in the 2014 PLOS Computational Biology study: NMP residues 30–67; LID 118–160; CORE 1–29, 68–117 and 161–214. Other studies use somewhat different boundaries. These labels are a declared convention, not a newly inferred segmentation.

Two snapshots are not a movie

Intermediate coordinates are linear geometric interpolation between aligned endpoints. They have no assigned physical time, energies, forces, kinetic rates or probability. Do not interpret them as molecular dynamics, a measured transition, an energetically valid path, or evidence for a binding mechanism.

Atomic bead radii and colors are display choices. Backbone mode uses a smooth curve through Cα positions; it does not infer secondary structure. Neither display is a solvent-accessible molecular surface. Crystal structures also reflect experimental conditions, crystal packing and coordinate uncertainty.

Independent numerical checks

The core test suite checks known rigid transformations, proper rotations versus reflections, malformed and sparse coordinates, source hashes, atom correspondence, domain counts, endpoint distances, and bounded interpolation.

An executed NumPy SVD comparison independently checks both real-structure fits and 30 seeded rigid-transform cases. It agrees with the JavaScript output to below 10⁻⁸ Å in those fixtures. These are numerical checks, not physical, mechanistic, clinical, or experimental validation.

The standalone src/science.mjs module uses no external packages. The built browser site also runs from any ordinary static web server. Optional NumPy verification requirements are pinned separately from the application.

Privacy & credits

No accounts, analytics, tracking SDKs or paid AI APIs are used. Local coordinate files and sequence construction stay on this device. Clicking a RCSB search or retrieval sends the explicit query or PDB ID to the official public service; sequence-builder text is not sent. Exports happen when you click download. A hosting provider receives ordinary page requests; source links open the referenced third-party sites.
